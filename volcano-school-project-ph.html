Four Scenes, One Volcano Model

Build the four sections and bring them together into a display that children can explain from different sides.

  • Inside a volcano: trace the route from magma to the crater and lava flow.
  • New land from lava: explore how cooled lava can build new land.
  • Rich volcanic soil: discuss how weathered volcanic material can contribute minerals to soil.
  • Eruption hazards: show how lava and other volcanic hazards can affect homes and the environment.

Explore How Volcanoes Change the Landscape

Mayon Volcano: A Philippine Example for Your School Model

Mayon is an active volcano in Albay, in the Bicol Region of Luzon. It gives Filipino students a real example to connect with the volcano parts and processes shown in their paper model.

Use the numbered cross section to point out magma below the surface, the main vent and the crater. Explain that molten rock is called lava when it reaches the surface. Then explore the other scenes to discuss how volcanic activity can form land, how weathered volcanic material can contribute to soil, and how eruptions can affect nearby communities.

This printable diorama is a general volcano teaching model, not an exact replica or scientific diagram of Mayon. Its four scenes illustrate different volcanic processes; they do not show four specific locations around Mayon.

A Simple Classroom Activity

  1. Find the Philippines, Luzon and Albay on a map. Mark the location of Mayon Volcano.
  2. Compare a photograph of Mayon with your paper model. Which features look similar? What details are different?
  3. Point to the numbered parts and explain the difference between magma and lava.
  4. Choose one landscape scene and explain what it teaches about volcanoes.

For official volcano information, explore the PHIVOLCS list of Philippine volcanoes. For current activity and safety advice, follow PHIVOLCS and local authorities.

How to Explain Your Volcano Model in Class

Children can use these short sentences as a starting point, then practise explaining the model in their own words:

“This is my 3D volcano model. The cutaway lets us see parts inside a volcano.”

“Molten rock below the surface is called magma. When it reaches the surface, we call it lava.”

“I can point to the main vent, the crater and the lava flow using the numbered labels.”

“Mayon in Albay is an active volcano in the Philippines. My model helps explain volcano parts, but it is not an exact copy of Mayon.”

“The other scenes show how volcanoes can create land, contribute to soil formation and cause hazards.”

For a short school presentation, choose two or three sentences and point to the matching parts as you speak. Check your teacher’s instructions for any extra facts or diagrams you need.
